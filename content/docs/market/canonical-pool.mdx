Burntato has one canonical native ETH/POTATO Uniswap v4 pool. Native ETH is
currency0, the Burntato Diamond is currency1, the native LP fee is zero, and
the Burntato hook supplies the 1% bilateral fee and POTATO transfer behavior.

## Structural identity

Before launch, authority can correct the market dependencies and reserved POTATO allocation. Launch then fixes the PoolKey, PoolManager, hook, tick spacing, range, allocation, and locked positions for that market.

Useful Diamond views are:

- `canonicalPoolKey()`
- `marketConfig()`
- `marketCurves()`
- `marketCurveHash()`
- `marketState()` and `marketReady()`
- `lockedLpRecipient()`

The hook separately exposes its token, PoolManager, tick spacing, owner, fee recipient, fee rate, Operator routing, deployment block, and external-buy state.

## Bilateral hook fee

The fee applies to both directions and is capped at 200 BPS by the installed hook.

- On a buy, the hook retains a fraction of gross POTATO output, converts it once to ETH without recursively charging its internal conversion, then allocates the realized ETH.
- On a sell, the hook retains a fraction of gross ETH output and allocates it directly.

The Operator share divides this existing fee; it does not add a second fee. Division dust goes to the hook's Treasury fee recipient.

Hook revenue bypasses Diamond claims and is not automatically added to liquidity, Winner, Recovery, or buyback accounting.

## How the market opens

The pool launches entirely POTATO-sided. Public buys remain closed while
protocol buybacks establish ETH-side liquidity. Exact-input POTATO sells can
operate once ETH inventory exists, and the canonical buyback uses a privileged
fee-free buy path.

Hook ownership controls the public-buy gate independently of Diamond
finalization.

## Swap constraints

The hook rejects foreign PoolKeys, foreign initialization, exact-output swaps, zero-amount swaps, and unauthorized liquidity addition. Integrations should use exact-input routes and refresh live hook state immediately before quoting.
